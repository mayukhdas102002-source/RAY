# 10 · Client communication

Clients approve what they understand in two minutes. Keep production detail (models, prompts, Remotion) out of client documents unless they ask.

## The client pack (in this order)

1. **Title and length.** "Homely · Going Up (54.5-second concept film)"
2. **Logline.** One or two sentences, present tense.
3. **The idea in one line.** Why this story sells the product.
4. **Shot by shot.** For each shot: number, name, time range, *what we see* (1–2 sentences), and *the message* (what it tells the viewer, with the feature in bold).
5. **Feature map.** Each promised feature points to the shot numbers that show it.
6. **Timing table** (when timing changes).
7. **Disclaimer.** Concept film, illustrative names and figures.

Template: `templates/client-shot-by-shot.md`. Worked example: `examples/homely-going-up/client-shot-by-shot.md`.

## Writing rules

- Plain words, short sentences. No jargon: "keyframe", "plate", "Seedance", "corner-pin", "LUT".
- Say what's on screen, not how it's made.
- One message per shot. Bold the feature name when a shot carries one.
- Put dialogue in quotes, exactly as it will be said.
- Use real numbers for timing (0:13–0:15.5), since clients give notes by time.

## Presenting options

- 3–6 loglines, ranked, each with the image, the line, and a one-sentence "why it wins".
- Recommend one, and say what the runner-up is better at.
- Ask the client to choose. Don't build three full packages.

## Handling feedback

- "Too dramatic": remove metaphors, return to one real moment, keep a single wow.
- "Feels stitched or abrupt": lengthen shots to 2.5–4s, add sound bridges, add 2–10s to the runtime.
- "Not clear": check the message hierarchy (one promise), put the product on a real screen, cut features back to the end card.
- "Not wow enough": strengthen the one image, not the number of effects.
