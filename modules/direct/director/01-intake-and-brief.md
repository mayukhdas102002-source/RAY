# 01 · Intake and brief

Most failed AI ads were briefed badly. Before any concept, get these written down. Ask for only what's missing, in one round, as multiple choice where you can. Research-style facts you can gather yourself (the product site, the reference ads the user attached) don't need a question.

## The brief card

| Field | What to capture | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Product | What it is, in one sentence a stranger understands | AI home-loan platform, NZ |
| Audience | Primary (who buys) and secondary (who else must be convinced) | First-home buyers; investors watching a pitch |
| **The one promise** | The single thing a viewer repeats afterwards | "A lending decision within 24 hours" |
| Proofs (≤2 on screen) | What makes the promise believable | Instant spending insight · lenders compete |
| Everything else | Features that go to the end card or deck, not the story | Marketplace, rewards, Open Finance… |
| Tone | Pick one: warm / witty / epic / deadpan / tender | Warm with one laugh |
| Length and versions | Master plus cutdowns | :55 master, :30, :15 |
| Aspect | 16:9 TV, 9:16 social, 1:1 feed | 16:9 (projected) |
| Where it plays | TV, pitch room, social, cinema, events | Laptop to TV, investor meeting |
| References | Ads they love and why, and ads they hate | Zillow :15 "Someday", Revolut |
| Must-haves | Logo, line, legal, product UI, local cues | Kiwi setting, "Concept film" disclaimer |
| Must-nots | Tone or brand risks | No mocking banks, no over-dramatic metaphors |
| Tools and budget | Which Higgsfield models and plan, free credits, who generates | Seedance 2.5, Kling 3.0, Minimax H3, Wan 3.0, Genjutsu free |
| Existing assets | Their frames, clips, brand kit, fonts, colours, UI | PDF keyframes, 3 clips, Homely orange |

## Message hierarchy (do this even if the client says "show all eight")

1. **Promise.** One line, customer benefit, testable.
2. **Proof 1.** A moment on screen that makes the promise true.
3. **Proof 2.** A second moment, of a different kind (emotional versus rational).
4. **Everything else.** It rides inside the story's engine or goes on the end card.

If the client needs every feature on screen (common for investor films), don't make a list. Pick a story engine that can visit each feature as a natural beat: a journey with stops, a ride with floors, a day with hours, an auction with bids. See `03-concept-and-story.md`.

## Questions worth asking (only the missing ones)

- Who is the one person in the film? Solo, couple, family? Do you have cast preferences?
- Is it realistic with one wow, or openly surreal? (Default: realistic with one wow.)
- Humour or heart?
- Is the product shown as today's app, or a future vision? (If future, label it "Concept film".)
- Can we use real brand names (banks, partners), or should they be fictional?
- Who presses Generate: us or you? (Be honest about what this session can execute.)

## Red flags to push back on (kindly)

- "Make it like X, Y and Z." Pick one reference for structure, and at most one for the wow.
- "Cover all eight features equally." Offer the story-engine approach, or a product card at the end.
- "Make it cinematic." Replace with the specific look: lens, light, grade, pace.
- "Use the same character and plot we already made" when the client has asked for a fresh start. Confirm which assets survive.
- Shots under 1.5s outside a deliberate montage. They read as stitched.
